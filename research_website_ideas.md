# External research notes for Rollz development ideas

## Sources

1. Chowly, “Restaurant Website Design: 7 Elements of a High-Converting Restaurant Website” (2026-01-28): https://chowly.com/resources/blogs/restaurant-website-design-7-elements-of-a-high-converting-restaurant-website/
2. Beyond Menu, “What Diners Expect When Ordering Online in 2026 (UX Insights)” (2026-02-24): https://get.beyondmenu.com/blog/restaurant-online-ordering-ux/
3. Baymard Institute, “Food Delivery & Takeout App & Website Ecommerce UX Research”: https://baymard.com/research/online-food-delivery

## Key findings used for recommendations

- Restaurant sites should be treated as direct ordering infrastructure, not only as visual brochures; success should be measured by completed orders and reduced friction.
- Mobile-first behavior is foundational: large thumb-friendly controls, readable menus without zooming, visible ordering access while scrolling, and fast loading are repeatedly emphasized.
- Practical ordering guidance includes HTML menu content, clear categories, visible prices, simple modifiers, transparent totals, minimal checkout steps, immediate confirmation, and no forced account creation.
- Location, phone, hours, map access, and ordering links should remain easy to find. The Rollz address must remain the verified address already in the project.
- Food visuals should reduce uncertainty and sit close to the menu/order action. Authentic imagery should be clearly distinguished from complementary illustrations.
- Trust is built through consistent branding, professional food presentation, clear pricing, immediate order confirmation, visible social proof only when verified, and security/reassurance signals.
- Retention opportunities include favorites, easy reorder, order history, and loyalty integration, but these should follow a frictionless first-order flow.
- Baymard frames food-delivery UX around homepage/category navigation, search, restaurant menus, item pages, cart/checkout, account/self-service, site-wide interaction, and loyalty/rewards. These categories are useful for organizing the 50 ideas.
- Motion should support comprehension and feedback rather than delay ordering: use short transitions, preserve clear CTA visibility, and respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Citation-ready claims

- Chowly states that high-converting restaurant sites prioritize mobile performance, visible ordering access, HTML menus, clear location/hours, useful visuals, clean layouts, and ongoing measurement.
- Beyond Menu states that current diners expect mobile-first ordering, fast-loading menus, scannable categories, transparent pricing, minimal checkout steps, immediate confirmation, trust signals, and easy reordering.
- Baymard reports a research framework spanning food browsing, customization, delivery/takeout ordering, cart/checkout, accounts, site-wide interaction, and loyalty, based on usability testing and benchmarking of major food-delivery services.
